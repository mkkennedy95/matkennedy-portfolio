I want to make worlds.
I want to create feelings.

Where This Started

I grew up in Sioux Falls, South Dakota — a shy kid who eventually became the class clown, and throughout all of it, someone who was always building something.

Writing was my first love. It was the first thing I was genuinely good at, and the first place I felt like myself. Whenever a class project gave me the option to make a video instead of writing a paper, I took it every time. I had this recurring fantasy of building worlds — little cities, dioramas, sandboxes. There was a project in school where we built an entire town out of cardboard and sand. It was my favorite thing I had ever done.

I think about that project a lot. Because that's still what I'm trying to do — build something from nothing, make it feel real, and invite people into it.

The Winding Road Here

The original dream was filmmaking. Then journalism — until I realized I didn't like the rules or the pay. Then advertising, specifically copywriting. I found my way to Creative Memories, a scrapbooking company, where I wrote product descriptions and brand copy across three markets simultaneously and learned what it meant to maintain a consistent voice at scale.

But I kept running into the same wall: I didn't want to write the blog posts. I wanted to make the Nike ads. I didn't want to manage the social calendar. I wanted to build the brand.

So I kept pushing. I pitched a video series at Creative Memories nobody asked for, produced it myself, and they put it in the email newsletter. I won a creator competition at a Midwest media company and spent a year as a one-person content studio — writing, filming, editing, producing a podcast, appearing on camera, and contributing to brand strategy, all at once. And then I walked into Matos Law Firm as a videographer and social media manager, and ended up building the entire marketing operation from scratch.

None of this was linear. But looking back, there's a through-line: I've never been able to just do the job in front of me. I always end up building something bigger than what I was asked for.

What I've Learned

I've spent most of my career working alone. One-person teams, figuring it out in real time, no playbook. For a long time I wasn't sure that made me capable. Now I know it does.

Working alone taught me to learn fast — to pick up enough about paid ads, community building, email strategy, operations, and hiring to do something real with all of it. But more than any specific skill, it taught me how to find the signal in the noise.

A lot of people say a lot of things about how marketing works. I've gotten better at knowing what actually matters: brand, story, and putting the customer first. Everything else is tactics.

Taste is everything. The people who win in the future are the ones with a clear vision of what people actually want — and the ability to execute on it.

That's who I'm working to become. And I think there has never been a better time to do it. With the tools available now, knowing what you want and knowing why you want it are the only questions that matter. If you have those answers, you can build almost anything.

Where I Am Now

I'm a brand and creative strategist based in Denver, Colorado. I'm currently the Marketing Director at Matos Law Firm, where I rebuilt the firm's entire growth engine from community up — cutting the budget by 80% and ending the year at virtually the same case volume. That case study lives in the Work section of this site.

I'm also building in public. I'm learning Meta advertising. I'm taking the Google Cloud Gen AI Leadership course. I'm vibe-coding my first app. I'm writing on LinkedIn and Substack about what I'm figuring out in real time — about AI, brand, creativity, and what it means to build a meaningful career when the rules keep changing.

Outside of work, I'm active — I work out, I explore Colorado, I take on side projects that interest me. I'm the kind of person who spends a lot of his free time working on his future, for better or for worse. I just need the right place to channel it.

If that sounds like someone you want on your team — or someone you want to work with — let's talk.
